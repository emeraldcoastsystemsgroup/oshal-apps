Purpose

As an Automation Process Engineer working in development, you will define how next-generation automated manufacturing processes must work. You will develop deep technical understanding of an assigned set of critical functions within a station, process area, or related sequence of operations, and translate that understanding into clear process requirements that guide concept selection, internal development, and downstream supplier implementation.


You will use engineering judgment, mechanistic reasoning, modeling, simulation, analogy to similar systems, and targeted experimentation to mature process understanding and reduce downstream risk. A major portion of this work is performed before production equipment exists and before empirical operating data is available. By the end of process development, you will be responsible for ensuring that your assigned scope is sufficiently understood and validated, and that a clear process specification exists defining what the production system must be designed to achieve.

Responsibilities

•    Develop deep technical understanding of an assigned set of critical functions within a station, process area, or related process sequence to define how material is transformed, positioned, presented, constrained, transferred, sequenced, measured, and verified through the automated process.
•    Generate and evaluate automation concepts from a process perspective and contribute to concept down-selection; and support internal development by providing clear process requirements and reviewing technical outputs against process success criteria.

•    Identify the key technical unknowns that must be resolved to support concept selection and process maturity, and close those gaps through expert input, engineering analysis, modeling, simulation, comparison to similar systems, and targeted experimentation.
•    Define station-level requirements for logistical material handling — transport, presentation, transfer, timing — that broader system handling must satisfy.
•    Tune, adjust, and align prototype and development equipment as needed to mature the process — using provided controls, software settings, hand tools, and alignment fixtures.
•    Convert validated learning into the process requirements and specifications that drive production system design and supplier implementation; and participate in review of supplier-proposed concepts and hardware selections where they affect the assigned scope.

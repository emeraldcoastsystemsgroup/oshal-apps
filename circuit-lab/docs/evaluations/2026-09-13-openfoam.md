# OpenFOAM (the list said "openflow") — evaluation (2026-09-13)

**What it is.** An open-source CFD toolbox. The OpenFOAM Foundation's licence page
(openfoam.org/licence, read 2026-09-13) states it is "freely available and open source, licensed
under the GNU General Public Licence", that "software that includes source code licensed under
the GPL inherits the GPL licence", that distributed binaries must come with source, and that the
licence "does not force users of the software to make modifications or developments publicly
available". Official docker images exist for both the Foundation and the ESI lines; the pages
listing them were not readable on 2026-09-13 (403 / 404), so names and sizes are *unverified*.

**The question.** Wall-clock and memory for the smallest useful case (a 2-D airfoil polar, then a
rotating propeller) on the box's CPU; whether XFOIL-class polars plus blade-element theory answer
the design question sooner.

**Answer.** The second half can be answered without a benchmark: the drone design (ADR-152)
needs thrust and torque vs rpm for a 6- and 7-inch propeller. Blade-element momentum theory with
section polars answers that in milliseconds and is what propeller design tools use for exactly
this; a rotating-propeller CFD case is a validation of that answer, not the way to get it. The
first half needs the run: a 2-D airfoil polar in OpenFOAM on this box is minutes to tens of
minutes per angle of attack at a usable mesh, a rotating propeller hours — figures that must be
measured here, not quoted.

| | Cost | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| OpenFOAM container | a GPL image built locally (never published); cases in minutes to hours; meshing is the real labour | a validation of the propeller curve and the airframe drag; field results for a renderer |
| BEMT + polars | a small pure-Python engine in aero-lab; polars from XFOIL (GPL, also container-local) or a published set | the design number in milliseconds, drivable by the concierge |
| Risk | mesh quality decides the answer; a wrong mesh is a confident wrong number | — |

**Verdict.** Blade-element theory first, in aero-lab (its backlog carries the propeller item);
the OpenFOAM benchmark is a BACKLOG item there, done when: a 2-D airfoil polar for the propeller
section runs in a local OpenFOAM container with the wall-clock and memory recorded per angle,
agrees with the XFOIL polar within the published tolerance for that section, and the result
feeds the BEMT curve as its check.

**Evidence.** https://openfoam.org/licence/ (fetched 2026-09-13); the docker image pages returned 403 / 404 on the day.

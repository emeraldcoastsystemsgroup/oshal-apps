# Calendar Preparation

The core Calendar is a workflow schedule view, and jarvis-overview calendar is empty; neither is a personal-event source. This package owns explicit primary-Google-calendar sync into a minimized caller-owned snapshot, protected by owner RLS. It preserves the existing core view. No sync runs on GET. Missing snapshot is Not synced, never zero. A saved snapshot contains at most 250 events in the next 30 days; counts are saved events, not a complete agenda. All-day dates retain their original date. Source sync time stays visible. Gift, meal, travel and meeting preparation use optional versioned app receivers. No attendees or descriptions are transferred, no occasion is inferred, and no booking or purchase is triggered. Destination apps own discussion, research and provider deep links. The caller connects Google through Identity and explicitly syncs; there is no deployment-token fallback.

A meeting brief is assembled in the background, not at request time. A framework-scope schedule calls one named handler behind a service route; for each upcoming meeting inside the lead window it assembles a brief from material the caller already recorded, delivers it through the registered Jarvis briefing source, and stores the delivered text. Consent is default-OFF and it is settled before anything is read: the source registers with enabled false, and the framework is asked whether it may deliver to that owner before a single record of theirs is opened, so an owner who has not turned it on gets no notification, no stored brief, and no reading of their recorded material. Every claim names the row that produced it - a prior brief for the same series, an ambient transcript segment captured inside that prior meeting's window, or the ambient daily review for that day. Material that cannot be cited is dropped; a meeting with no recorded material says "No prior context recorded for this meeting." Ambient transcripts and reviews are read only when ambient capture is enabled for that owner, and only for voices that owner's per-speaker consent settings include: the framework's own ADR-100 consent gate decides, so a person who declined, was never asked, is a minor, or cannot be attributed to a voice is not cited at all. Recorded words are cited, never copied. A brief carries the row id and the time, not the sentence: a copy of the words would live on in the delivered notification and in the stored brief, and neither of those is reached by the retention window, the delete-this-day control, the ambient erasure path, or a consent withdrawn later - so the words stay where those controls can still reach them. A brief states plainly that it holds no attendees, that recorded conversation is cited rather than copied, that only consenting voices are cited, and, when capture is off, that recorded conversation was not read at all. No model is called on this path. The surface shows the stored delivery text unchanged, so what is read and what was delivered are the same bytes.

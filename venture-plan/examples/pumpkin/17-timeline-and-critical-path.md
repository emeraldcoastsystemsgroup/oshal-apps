## The dates that are not in the engine

**The retail calendar is the binding constraint on the whole venture and it is not in the production schedule at all.**

- Halloween category review windows relative to the selling season: Roughly 9 to 12 months ahead. A decision made in October is for NEXT year's set. This is the schedule driver for the entire venture. It means the earliest realistic big-box shelf date for a product not yet designed is two seasons out.
- Lead time to reach out ahead of a retailer category review window: 3–4 months. Trade guidance - showing up after the window means waiting a full year.
- Consumer purchase window for Halloween decorations: Late August through September; decorations lead the season and the widest selection is bought before October.

Compounded, the dataset puts first contact for a big-box set two Halloweens out for a product that does not yet exist. And the harder constraint is not the calendar: A first-time vendor with no listing, no certification, no factory audit and no production history is not a candidate for a category review. The realistic sequence is: prove sell-through in CH-1/CH-2 for one season, then pitch with data.

**That is the schedule reason the sequencing in the decision summary is what it is**, and it holds regardless of what the cost model says. Even if every cost band landed at its best corner tomorrow, the shelf is not available for two seasons.

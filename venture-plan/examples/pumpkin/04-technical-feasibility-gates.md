## The gates

### TG-1 — Does the projector geometry close inside the envelope?

**Severity:** product-killer-if-unresolved

```
throwDistanceIn = throwRatio * imageWidthIn; PASS if throwDistanceIn <= usableInternalPathIn
```

| Case | throwDistanceIn | usableIn | marginIn | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cheap 1.2:1 projector, 28 in face, 34 in horizontal path | 33.6 | 34 | 0.4 | FAIL - 0.4 in of margin is not a design margin, it is a coincidence |
| cheap 1.5:1 projector, 28 in face, 34 in horizontal path | 42 | 34 | -8 | FAIL |
| short-throw 0.8:1, 28 in face, 34 in horizontal path | 22.4 | 34 | 11.6 | PASS |
| short-throw 0.5:1, 28 in face, 34 in horizontal path | 14 | 34 | 20 | PASS |
| cheap 1.5:1 firing UP the long axis to an angled panel, 28 in face, 60 in path | 42 | 60 | 18 | PASS - but introduces a keystone/focus-plane problem |


**Verdict.** CONDITIONAL PASS. A standard 1.2-1.5:1 mini projector does NOT close across the width of a 6 ft envelope at a 28 in face. Two ways out: buy a short-throw lens (unproven at this price point - A-GEO-3 needsQuote) or fire up the long axis and correct keystone (free in BOM, costs mechanical design). The incumbent's existence at 99.99 USD retail (C-1) is evidence the long-axis approach works.

**Resolved by.** Ask three mini-projector suppliers for a 0.5-0.8 throw ratio unit at 5000 pcs with a measured ANSI lumens test report. If none exists under 90 USD, commit to the long-axis geometry and prototype the keystone quality.

### TG-2 — Is the projected face actually visible in an outdoor yard?

**Severity:** product-killer-if-it-fails


**Verdict.** PASS after dark, FAIL before dark. The worst-case corner of the input bands still gives 32:1 contrast in a dark yard, so this is robust rather than marginal. Failing in daylight is normal for the whole category and is not a defect - but the packaging and listing copy must say 'after dark' plainly, because a customer who unboxes it at 4pm on Halloween and sees nothing will return it.

**Resolved by.** Nothing. This gate is closed by arithmetic on researched bands. Confirm with a measured panel transmittance once a sample exists.

### TG-3 — Does a cheap projector's poor black level show a visible glowing rectangle on the pumpkin?

**Severity:** would-look-cheap-and-wrong

```
blackIllum_lux = E_full_lux / contrastRatio; blackLuminance_nits = blackIllum_lux * panelTransmittance / PI; INVISIBLE if blackLuminance_nits < ambientFabricLuminance_nits
```

| Case | blackIllum_lux | blackLuminance_nits | ambientFabricLuminance_nits | ratio | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 250 ANSI, contrast 800:1, tau 0.25, 5 lux ambient | 0.925 | 0.074 | 0.8 | 0.09 | INVISIBLE - projected black is 11x DIMMER than the ambient-lit fabric around it |
| 250 ANSI, contrast 200:1 (worst band), tau 0.25, 5 lux ambient | 3.7 | 0.295 | 0.8 | 0.37 | INVISIBLE - still below the ambient-lit fabric |
| 250 ANSI, contrast 200:1, tau 0.25, 1 lux ambient (very dark rural yard) | 3.7 | 0.295 | 0.159 | 1.85 | FAINTLY VISIBLE - a dim rectangle in a very dark yard at the worst contrast corner |


**Verdict.** PASS, and this is a case where doing the arithmetic overturned the intuition. The obvious fear - that a cheap projector paints a grey box on the pumpkin - is wrong in a typical suburban yard because the shipped preset renders a PURE BLACK background (SW-2), so the black field is at 0.07 to 0.30 nits against fabric that ambient light already raises to 0.80 nits. Only at the worst contrast corner in a very dark rural yard does a faint rectangle appear, and a projected soft-edge vignette in the renderer removes even that.

**Resolved by.** Specify contrast >= 400:1 measured on/off in the projector quote, and add a soft-edged vignette to the 'inflatable' preset. Both are cheap.

**Software action.** Add a radial soft-edge vignette to the inflatable preset so the frame boundary is never a hard edge. This is a small change to the existing renderer, not new work.

### TG-4 — Does a projector running for six hours cook the inside of the inflatable?

**Severity:** feared-but-resolvable

```
heat_BTUhr = projectorWatts * heatFraction * 3.412; deltaT_F = heat_BTUhr / (1.08 * CFM)
```

| Case | heat_BTUhr | deltaT_F | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 45 W projector, 200 CFM blower | 145.9 | 0.68 | PASS - negligible |
| 60 W projector (high band), 100 CFM blower (low band) | 194.5 | 1.8 | PASS |
| 60 W projector, 50 CFM effective through the projector zone | 194.5 | 3.6 | PASS |


**Verdict.** PASS. A cold-air inflatable is not sealed - the blower runs continuously and the fabric leaks by design (A-THM-3), so the interior is a moving air stream, not an oven. Even a pessimistic 50 CFM through the projector zone gives under 4 degF of bulk rise. The residual risk is LOCAL and mechanical, not thermodynamic: fabric collapsing onto the projector's intake or exhaust when the blower cycles or the prop is knocked. B-09's cage exists for exactly that.

**Resolved by.** Nothing thermodynamic. Validate the mechanical case with a six-hour soak test on the first prototype, instrumented at the projector exhaust, with the fabric deliberately pushed against the housing.

### TG-5 — Does weather and moisture kill it overnight?

**Severity:** likely-warranty-driver

**Verdict.** OPEN AND UNRESOLVED. The same continuous airflow that solves the thermal gate is the moisture problem: the blower pulls outdoor air, including humidity, dew and blown rain, straight past the optics for six hours. A warm lens in cold humid air fogs; a cooling lens after shutdown condenses. This is the most likely source of field failures and returns and no arithmetic in this file resolves it.

**Resolved by.** Prototype an overnight dew-cycle test. Design candidates: sealed optical window with the projector's own cooling loop kept separate from the envelope air, a hydrophobic window coating, a small resistive window heater, and an orientation that keeps the window facing down-and-forward so condensate runs off.

### TG-7 — Can it hear a child over the blower, outdoors?

**Severity:** core-feature-risk

**Verdict.** OPEN. The blower is a continuous broadband noise source located inside the same enclosure as the microphone, roughly 2 to 4 ft away, running the entire time the prop is up. The software already handles the OTHER hard audio problem well - it hard-stops the mic before its own speech, holds a 900 ms reverb window and rejects self-echo by word overlap (SW-6) - but nothing addresses blower noise, and no measurement exists.

**Resolved by.** Measure SPL at the intended mic position with the blower running. If SNR is inadequate, the fixes are ordered by cost: mic placement outside the envelope on the base, a directional or beamforming array, a push-to-talk button on the prop, or dropping open-mic and shipping the phone remote (SW-4) as the only input path.

## What the gates mean for the cost model

**The geometry gate is a cost question wearing a physics costume.** The projector is modelled at $56.50, inside a $28.00–$85.00 band that assumes a mid-range LCD class. The dataset's own note is that this class is built around a 1.2–1.5:1 throw ratio, which does not close the geometry — and that if a short-throw lens is only available in the higher module tier, the line roughly triples. For scale: on the big-box configuration, contribution reaches zero when this line falls to $39.91. Moving it the other way ends the tier.

**The visibility gate closes on arithmetic and needs a separate part to do it.** The projection panel is a distinct line at $6.00, not the envelope fabric, and the dataset is explicit that standard coated oxford is far too opaque to rear-project through. It is a small number attached to a total product failure if it is dropped as a cost-down.

**The thermal gate passes and the moisture gate does not, for the same reason.** The continuous blower airflow that keeps the interior within a degree of ambient is the same airflow that pulls dew and rain past the optics for six hours. The enclosure line carries $9.00 of unit cost and $19,000.00 of tooling against a problem no arithmetic in this plan resolves.

**The streaming question resolves against streaming.** Moving the renderer inside the prop and sending only text and audio over the network is what puts the compute module in the bill of materials at $28.50, and it is the second-largest driver in the sensitivity chart. The alternative — casting pixels from a phone — would remove that cost and break the audio-driven lip sync that is the entire product.

## What already ships, and what it means

### Gemmy — $99.99

**Product.** Projection Airblown Inflatable, Fire and Ice Frightening Pumpkin Giant Scene, 12 ft

**What it does.** An internally-lit inflatable with a rotating kaleidoscope LED projection unit throwing swirling colour onto the skin. Also sold as a standalone Fire and Ice projection spotlight accessory.

**What it does not do.** It projects a colour pattern, not a rendered face. It does not lip-sync, does not listen, does not speak, and does not respond.

**Why that is structural rather than a feature gap.** The gap is not a feature Gemmy has declined to add - it is a different product architecture. A rotating gobo has no frame buffer, no audio pipeline and no compute, so no amount of iteration on that light unit produces a face that talks. Closing the gap means adding a video engine, a renderer, an audio path and a network stack - which is the BOM difference between their 99.99 USD unit and this venture's V-A.

**What it proves for this venture.** This is the most valuable competitive fact in the file, and it is good news. It establishes that a projector inside a cold-air inflatable, outdoors, for a full Halloween season, at a 99.99 USD RETAIL price, is a solved manufacturing problem. The geometry, the thermal behaviour, the weather tolerance and the retail acceptance are all demonstrated by a shipping product. This venture is not inventing the physical form factor - it is putting a different light engine inside a proven one.

**What it costs this venture.** It also sets the price anchor on the inflatables shelf at 99.99 USD. V-A cannot be merchandised there.

### AtmosFX (and licensed kit assemblers such as HoliScapes) — $229.99

**Product.** Digital decoration content plus projector-and-screen kits, including Jack-O-Lantern Jamboree

**What it does.** Pre-rendered video loops projected onto a flat translucent screen in a window or doorway. Very high production quality.

**What it does not do.** The content is pre-rendered video. It cannot respond to a specific child, cannot say a name, cannot answer a question, and its mouth is not driven by anything that is happening in the yard.

**Why that is structural rather than a feature gap.** A pre-rendered library and a real-time procedural renderer are different things. A video file cannot lip-sync to a sentence that did not exist when the file was made; the shipped renderer here drives the mouth from the live RMS of the audio it is speaking (SW-3). That difference is architectural, not a matter of adding more loops to the library.

**What it proves for this venture.** It brackets what a projector-plus-screen Halloween bundle sells for direct, at 229.99-414.99 USD depending on the surface - a band that straddles the V-A target rather than sitting above it - and it validates a 24.99 USD price for content alone, which is the V-C comparable.

**What it costs this venture.** It sets a very high production-quality bar for the visuals. A procedural face has to look deliberately stylised and good, not like a cheaper version of rendered video.

### The animatronics shelf generally — $169.99 – $399.99

**Product.** Motion- and sound-activated animated props (skeletons, dolls, groundbreakers)

**What it does.** Triggered by a motion sensor, plays one of a small set of canned audio lines with a servo-driven motion sequence.

**What it does not do.** It plays back; it does not converse. The line it says is unrelated to anything the visitor said.

**Why that is structural rather than a feature gap.** This is the shelf V-A must be merchandised on and the price band it must land in. The differentiation claim that survives scrutiny is narrow and true: the props on this shelf respond to PRESENCE, this one can respond to WORDS. State it that way and it is defensible.

## The differentiation claim that survives scrutiny

**"The props on this shelf respond to presence. This one can respond to words."** 

That claim is narrow, it is true, and it is checkable. It does not say anybody else is incapable of building this; it says what the difference is and why it is architectural: Lip-sync driven by real per-frame RMS of the OUTPUT audio (WebAudio AnalyserNode), not a canned mouth loop — pumpkin-audio.js speak() -> AnalyserNode -> amplitude callback. A pre-rendered library and a real-time procedural renderer are different things. A video file cannot lip-sync to a sentence that did not exist when the file was made; the shipped renderer here drives the mouth from the live RMS of the audio it is speaking (SW-3). That difference is architectural, not a matter of adding more loops to the library.

**What that claim does not do is carry a price.** The animatronics shelf tops out at an observed $399.99, and at that price the self-contained unit loses $21.37 per unit. A better story does not move a shelf ceiling; it moves sell-through at a price the shelf already supports.

**And the demonstration risk runs the other way.** A pre-rendered video library sets a high production-quality bar. A procedural face has to look deliberately stylised and good rather than like a cheaper version of rendered video, and no part of this plan prices that design work.

Soil temperature field — two years by depth

Colour is temperature, diverging about the soil mean, so the neutral midpoint reads as "nothing to harvest here". Two things are visible in one frame. Damping: the annual wave fades toward the midpoint with depth — at the default soil its damping depth is 2.24 m, so 4 m down it retains under a fifth of the surface swing. Phase lag: the pale contour is the soil-mean isotherm, and it tilts steadily right with depth because deeper soil crosses its own mean later — roughly a season behind by 3 m. That lag, not the geothermal gradient, is what gives a buried pair any dT at all. The lower strip re-zooms to 14 days and 0.6 m, where the 24-hour wave is resolvable and dies out by about half a metre.

dT across each junction pair — and the windows where it yields nothing

The design story. Pair B is driven by the annual wave alone: its dT sweeps through zero twice a year, and because harvested power goes as dT squared, it sits below the converter's cut-in for weeks each time — the shaded windows. (They fall near the equinoxes, offset by the depth-dependent phase lag rather than landing exactly on them.) Pair A is driven by the diurnal wave, drawn as a min/max envelope because its daily swing is far faster than one pixel column here; it nulls twice a day, for about an hour. The two never null together, which is why wiring both into one converter collapses the worst-case gap from weeks to hours.

Thermal impedance match

Heat has to flow to be converted, and only the fraction of dT that falls across the module does any work. Sweeping the module's thermal resistance against power at pair B's peak dT gives a curve that peaks exactly where the module matches the soil path. Raw dT is the environment's to give; the match is yours to choose.

Marine reference domain — a tidal node over one spring/neap beat

The same grammar on the other environment, so the comparison is between the domains rather than between two chart styles. Flow speed is drawn as a min/max envelope because the 12.42-hour carrier is far faster than a pixel column — and that envelope is the spring/neap beat, which nobody sets: it emerges from the lunar and solar semidiurnal constituents drifting in and out of phase every 14.77 days. Note the contrast worth taking away: this node harvests watts where the buried one harvests microwatts, but it is bound by exactly the same rule — the neap minimum sets the store, not the thirty-day mean. The model

Soil. The harmonic solution to the one-dimensional heat equation in a semi-infinite solid: T(z,t) = T_mean + A·exp(−z/d)·cos(ωt − z/d), with damping depth d = √(2α/ω). The annual and diurnal waves superpose, each with its own surface amplitude and its own damping depth (2.24 m and 0.117 m at α = 0.5×10⁻⁶ m²/s). The geothermal gradient is included as a linear term at 0.025 K/m and is kept in the table precisely to show how small it is: a three-metre probe sees 0.075 K, against an annual swing of several kelvin. It is a documented finding, not an omission.

Harvester. A thermal resistance network first — R_soil = L/(k·A), Q = |dT|/(R_soil + R_teg), and only dT_module = Q·R_teg falls across the module — then the standard figure-of-merit efficiency at ZT̄ = 0.9 with temperatures in kelvin. Sign-agnostic, because the junction polarity reverses every half-cycle and the converter bridges it. Budget. A closed-loop integration: "perpetual" requires both that the pack never browned out and that the run ended no worse charged than it started, so a design that merely drains slowly is correctly rejected.

Provenance. Site climates, soils and the marine site are plausible illustrative parameters over a real model — not a survey, not harmonic constants for any station. Replace them with measured values before siting anything. When /api/ocean-lab/harvest is reachable its sites are merged into the picker and its verdict appears as a cross-check row in the table; the headline verdict is always the one computed here, so the page cannot contradict itself.

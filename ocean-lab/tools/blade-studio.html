A rotor blade, solved rather than drawn. The blade you see is the blade the solver used and the blade the export writes, so what prints is what was analysed.

Low-Reynolds warning. The section at 75 % span is running at Re —, below about 1×10⁵. The polar is outside its reliable range at this scale. A NACA 4-digit aerofoil was drawn for Reynolds numbers an order of magnitude higher; down here a laminar separation bubble dominates, lift-to-drag collapses into the low twenties, and the drag figures on this page are correlations rather than a solved boundary layer. Treat the Cp below as an upper bound and expect a real print to under-perform it. Shrinking a utility-scale design point onto a print bed is the classic small-rotor mistake; this is where it shows up.

The blade, in three dimensions

The lofted solid itself — one NACA section per design station, scaled by that station's chord and rotated about the quarter-chord by its total blade angle, then capped at both ends so the result is a closed solid rather than an open shell. Flat shading with real facet normals, because a smoothed normal averages across the leading edge and hides the one thing worth seeing: the twist, which reads as the steady change in facet brightness from the thick root to the thin tip. Every blade is drawn, so blade count is visible too. The floor is a 10 mm build-plate lattice with every tenth line at 100 mm. Drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom, arrow keys to rotate.

Power coefficient against tip-speed ratio

The rotor's whole character in one curve. Cp rises as the blades start doing work, peaks where the twist schedule matches the inflow, and falls again once the sections stall or the drag penalty outgrows the lift. The Betz limit is drawn as a hard ceiling: 16/27 is what momentum theory permits any open-flow rotor, and a curve that crossed it would be a solver bug, not a discovery — which is exactly why the Glauert/Buhl correction above a = 0.4 is not optional. The marked point is where the operating tip-speed-ratio control currently sits, and it is the Cp that feeds the energy verdict at the foot of the page.

Spanwise distributions

What each annulus of the blade is actually doing at the operating point, hub to tip. Six panels rather than one chart with six lines: angle of attack, lift, drag, lift-to-drag, Reynolds and the induction factors carry five different units and five different magnitudes, and overlaying them would need a second y-axis — which is never the answer. Read them together. α falls outboard because the twist takes it out; Reynolds rises outboard because rotational speed dominates the relative wind, so the tip is the only part of a small blade flying in a regime the aerofoil recognises; a should sit near ⅓ over the working span (that is the Betz optimum) and collapse at both ends, which is the Prandtl tip and hub loss doing its job. Each panel is labelled at 75 % span — the station a rotor is designed around, and the one the low-Reynolds warning is taken at.

The closed loop — what the blade is worth to a node that has to live out there

This is the point of the whole page. The Cp above is not a score; it is an input. It becomes a harvest curve against flow speed, that curve is integrated against a real tidal schedule for a full spring/neap beat, and the result decides how big a battery the node needs. Note what the flow envelope does without anyone setting it: the beat is emergent, from the lunar and solar semidiurnal constituents drifting in and out of phase every 14.77 days. Sizing off the thirty-day mean is the classic error — the binding constraint is the longest stretch the environment gives the node nothing, which is a property of the schedule, not of its average. The model

Section. NACA 4-digit in closed form, with the −0.1036 thickness coefficient so the trailing edge closes exactly — the −0.1015 form of the original report leaves a 0.2 %c gap that lofts into a slot down the blade and will not slice. Chordwise stations are cosine-spaced. Lift comes from a Hess-Smith vortex panel solution and is therefore solved; because the panel matrix does not depend on the angle of attack, two solves characterise the section exactly as Cl(α) = A cos α + B sin α. Drag comes from correlations and is therefore an estimate: skin friction with a Hoerner thickness factor, a pressure term, a drag bucket, and a laminar-separation penalty below Re ≈ 2×10⁵ that is deliberately not clamped.

Rotor. Blade element momentum theory, solved by bracketed bisection on the inflow angle rather than fixed-point iteration on the induction factors: the residual is a function of φ alone, it changes sign inside the bracket, so a root exists and bisection cannot fail to find it. Prandtl tip and hub losses are both applied (the hub form divides by the hub radius, not the local radius — dividing by r under-penalises exactly the thick structural root a printed blade has). Above a = 0.4 momentum theory is invalid in the turbulent wake state and the Buhl fit takes over. An element that does not converge is reported as failed and contributes zero, so a partial solve looks wrong instead of looking optimistic.

Budget. A closed-loop integration over 720 hours: "perpetual" requires both that the pack never browned out and that the run ended in net energy surplus once charging losses are paid, so a design that merely drains slowly is correctly rejected. The minimum store is a binary search over capacity with each trial started at the design's own state of charge.

Provenance and honesty. The site harmonics, the load set and the store are plausible illustrative parameters over a real model — not a survey and not harmonic constants for any station. To stay live under a slider this page solves 24 blade elements and 40 panel stations per surface where the server uses 48 and 80; that moves Cp by well under a percent and is precisely why the server's own answer is shown beside this one rather than instead of it. When the API is reachable, /api/ocean-lab/rotor/solve and /api/ocean-lab/harvest/simulate appear as a cross-check row in the table and in the mode badge — the headline verdict is always the one computed here, so the page cannot contradict itself.
